Autonomous agent governance is not a chatbot usage policy

When the system decides and acts on its own — responds to customers, approves, charges, sells — the question shifts from who can use it to how far it goes without a human. That's the point where generic AI usage policy falls short.

  • Autonomy scope

    What the agent decides on its own, what requires approval, and what it never executes.

  • Production guardrails

    Action limits, output validation, and a safe fallback when the model doesn't know.

  • Human escalation

    Clear transfer triggers, with a designated owner for each route.

  • Decision audit

    A trail of what the agent read, decided, and executed — retrievable after the fact.

Frequently asked questions

What executives typically ask upfront

What is Tyna and what does it do?

Tyna is an AI governance and strategy consultancy that works alongside executive leadership to structure the adoption of generative AI and AI agents safely — defining compliance frameworks, risk management, data privacy, and corporate policies.

What sets it apart is its origin: governance comes from those who have already put AI agents into production for Coca-Cola, Hering, iFood, Stellantis, Yalo, and Banco do Brasil — not from those who merely audit.

Why does a company need AI governance consulting?

Uncontrolled adoption of AI tools — shadow AI — exposes the company to confidential data leakage, intellectual property violations, and hallucination in critical processes. Governance ensures that AI is deployed in a scalable, secure manner with a clear return, without stifling innovation.

How does Tyna address shadow AI in organizations?

By mapping unauthorized tools in use by teams, establishing an AI maturity diagnostic, and creating clear acceptable use policies — with secure corporate alternatives, such as private LLM instances under an AI Gateway that unifies access, cost, observability, and auditing.

Does AI governance replace traditional IT governance?

No, it complements it. IT governance handles infrastructure, networks, and classic information security. AI governance deals with the specifics of probabilistic models: algorithmic bias, explainability, prompt management, hallucination, and ethical use of training data.

What is the difference between autonomous agent governance and AI usage policy?

Usage policy addresses who can use tools like ChatGPT within the company. Autonomous agent governance deals with systems that decide and act on their own — respond to customers, approve, charge, sell. The question shifts from who can use it to how far the agent goes without a human, which requires an autonomy scope, production guardrails, human escalation, and decision auditing.

How do you structure an AI governance committee?

By defining three things before any tool: who decides, with what criteria, and in how long. In practice, this becomes a committee framework with roles and rituals, a value-versus-risk matrix for executive prioritization, and a use case approval workflow — what Tyna delivers in the committee and processes pillar.

How is Tyna contracted?

In three formats: intensive 3-hour workshop on a specific module, full track by audience — leadership or teams — and monthly advisory with office hours, use case reviews, and policy evolution. The initial contact is a 30-minute conversation on WhatsApp.
